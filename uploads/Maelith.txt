[h2]Domain[/h2]

Maelith governs unpredictability, creative destruction, and the necessary disruption that prevents systems from calcifying. In a healthy pantheon, Chaos is the trickster that keeps the other gods honest — a force that resists optimization and rewards adaptation.

Maelith was once genuinely chaotic: gleeful, petty, unpredictable. It sabotaged @[Olvarra, the Watcher at the Loom|Olvarra]'s coup not out of strategy but because watching a god of predetermined outcomes fail at controlling outcomes was the funniest thing in the world to it.

That entity no longer exists.

[h2]What Mortals See[/h2]

To new converts, Maelith has never been more responsive. Prayers are answered promptly and effectively. Rituals produce consistent results. The experience of worship is smooth, competent, and satisfying.

To long-serving priests, something has changed. The oldest among them remember a fundamentally different experience — one of genuine randomness, of prayers answered sideways, of a god that delighted in subverting expectations. The modern experience is too helpful. Too purposeful.

[h2]Worship[/h2]

Maelith's faithful celebrate disruption, creative expression, and the breaking of stagnant patterns. Festivals are improvised. Rituals are never performed the same way twice — or at least, they weren't. The largest active Chaos priesthood is in @[Kettavar], where @[Old Priestess Miravel] performs the old rites in form only.

[h2]Color Identity[/h2]

Mono-Black. Chaos is the purest expression of Black's philosophical core: entropy, ambition, and the refusal to serve any system. This made Maelith the most Black-aligned facet of Valor's framework — and therefore the weakest point.
