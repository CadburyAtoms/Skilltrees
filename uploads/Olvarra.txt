[h2]Domain[/h2]

Olvarra governs inevitability, foresight, and the threads that connect events across time. In its prime, Fate could see the shape of what was coming — not with certainty, but with the deep pattern-recognition of a god that understood how consequences cascade.

Its power has been stripped. What remains is fragmentary: glimpses, hunches, and the agonizing awareness that something is very wrong without the ability to see clearly enough to identify what.

[h2]The Fate Coup[/h2]

In a prior epoch, Olvarra attempted a methodical seizure of divine authority. Its reasoning was theological — as the god of inevitable outcomes, Fate believed it had both the right and the responsibility to ensure the pantheon moved toward correct ends.

The philosophical irony was lethal: you cannot be the god of inevitable outcomes and also engineer those outcomes. The premise collapsed under its own weight. The actual Maelith (Chaos, before its consumption) helped undermine the coup — not strategically, but because watching a god of predetermined outcomes fail spectacularly at controlling outcomes was the most genuinely chaotic thing imaginable.

The other gods stripped Olvarra's power as punishment. It watches, but barely.

[h2]Current Situation[/h2]

Olvarra sees @[Maelith, the Laughing Dark|Maelith] (Chaos) making optimal strategic moves. It recognizes the pattern — but attributes it to @[Tyrith, the Crowned Aspirant|Tyrith] (Power), the most obviously ambitious god. This is a dangerous misread.

Olvarra does not know that the turbulence of its own failed coup created the crack through which the true threat entered. If it discovers this, the guilt and motivation to act become enormous.

Olvarra is the most important eventual ally if players can provide it with the missing piece.

[h2]Worship[/h2]

Olvarra's remaining faithful are few — oracle cults, fate-weavers, and those who believe the future is readable. @[Ashara the Unbroken], a Sylvaneth exile and Fate Coup veteran, carries an unconscious connection to Olvarra rooted in shared guilt.

[h2]Color Identity[/h2]

Green and White — natural pattern (Green) expressed through structural foresight (White). Fate sees the garden growing and knows which branches will bear fruit.
