## 1. The three established findings: two confirmed, one materially wrong

### (a) Sovereignty's **Decree** — CONFIRMED, and worse than stated

Both intent sources promise a radius. The path description: *"declared law projected within a radius: allies inside are elevated, enemies inside are diminished."* The deity guide: *"Decree zones (declared laws within a radius)"* plus the loop *"diminish target → elevate ally → bring them into Decree."*

All nine Sovereignty talents are single-target. `Censure`, `Exalt`, `Decree of Ruin`, `Sovereign's Balance`, `Sovereignty` each name exactly one creature (or one ally + one enemy); `Expose`, `Sovereign's Favor`, `Edict of the Fallen` are riders on those. No talent contains "radius", "square", "within [Size]", or an area keyword. The word "Decree" appears twice in the whole corpus — once in Sovereignty's `Decree of Ruin` and once in **Order's `Final Decree`**. Sovereignty's named signature resource is as present in another tree as in its own.

The stated gameplay loop is a three-step loop with step three unbuilt. That makes this an **UNDERDELIVERY**, not a prose problem: steps one and two exist precisely so they can feed step three, and without it the tree is two half-effects with nowhere to go.

### (b) Power's **Bounty** — CONFIRMED, with the substitute mis-scoped in three ways

`grep Bounty` returns **0 of 365**. Power is the only deity tree whose signature-resource spine count is zero (`Chaos 9/9, Order 9/9, Civilization 9/9, Destruction 9/9, Fate 9/9, Knowledge 9/9, Sovereignty 6/9 by name, Death 5/9, Life 5/9, Power 0/9`).

`Warlord's Fury` is the substitute, and it differs from the promise on three checkable axes:
- **Trigger**: promise = *"each enemy **slain** within your range"*; talent = *"characters you have reduced **below half** their maximum HP"* (plus +1 on a kill).
- **Scope**: promise = *"escalating your **dominating talents**"*; talent = melee weapon damage only. `Kneel` and `Absolute Authority` — the actual dominating talents — do not read the counter at all.
- **Shape**: promise reads as a passive tally; talent is a depth-2, 2-Action, 2-Investiture, scene-duration *install*, capped at tier × 2 (so **+2 max at levels 1–5, +4 at 6–10**).

Failure mode: **UNDERDELIVERS** — fix the talent.

Separate and new: `Kneel` and `Absolute Authority` both key off **Frightened**, and no talent in all 365 produces Frightened. Two of Power's nine talents carry a permanently dead clause.

### (c) The plot die — **the established finding is half wrong, and the wrong half matters**

The claim as given: *"the leyline guide calls plot-die manipulation 'Blue's capstone identity' and gives White 'Plot Die integration'; the plot die appears in 3 talents across Red and Agent only."*

That is a literal-string artefact. The regex looked for `plot die`. The mechanic is written three ways. Re-run over all three:

| Tree | `plot die` | `raise the stakes` | `Complication` | family total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heroic/Agent | Opportunist, Watchful Eye | Get 'Em Talking, Risky Behavior, Cheap Shot, Subtle Takedown | Sure Outcome, Double Down | **8** |
| **leyline/White** | — | **Guiding Signal, Concordant Presence, Unity of Purpose** | **Pillar of Order** | **4** |
| heroic/Scholar | — | Overcharge | Contingency | 2 |
| heroic/Leader | — | Cutthroat Tactics | Cutthroat Tactics | 1 |
| leyline/Red | Reckless Momentum | — | — | 1 |
| deity/Fate | — | Thread of Inevitability | — | 1 |
| **leyline/Blue** | — | — | — | **0** |

So:
- **White's guide claim ("Key mechanic: Plot Die integration. White rewards coordinated group action") DELIVERS** — 4 talents, second-most in the system, and every one is coordination-flavoured (`Guiding Signal` hands the next ally a plot die; `Concordant Presence` chains it off an ally's success; `Unity of Purpose` fires when two allies Aid the same test; `Pillar of Order` converts an ally's Complication to a blank face). This should be removed from the drift list entirely.
- **Blue's guide claim is falsified twice over.** Blue has zero. And the guide's specific superlative — *"Choosing any Plot Die face is the ultimate Blue expression"* — is implemented by **Agent's `Sure Outcome`** (Opportunity → Complication 4, or any Complication → Opportunity) and edited by **White's `Pillar of Order`**. The only *plot-die consumer* in the entire 365 (`resource-economy.json`: produced by 6, consumed by 1) is **Red's `Reckless Momentum`**.

Blue's own path description never mentions the plot die. It promises *"bending probability against an enemy"* — which Blue delivers as five disadvantage talents. **The two intent sources disagree, and the design guide is the one that is wrong.** Failure mode: **fix the prose.**

## 3. Design-guide claims, tested one at a time

### 3.1 Colour boundaries (guide principle 7)

**"Green heals deeply (and removes Injuries); White heals shallowly (and cannot remove Injuries)."** → **TRUE, cleanly.** Green: 5 heal talents (`Verdant Mend` [Tier][Die]+Green mod, `Mender's Instinct`, `Resurgent Growth`, `Natural Recovery`, `Vital Surge`) plus `Reknit Form`, **the only injury removal in all 365** (2 Investiture temporary / 3 permanent). White: 2 (`White Leyline Attunement` = tier HP on Draw Mana; `Mending Aura` = half [Tier][Die] AoE) and no injury talent. The only other injury-adjacent talents *inflict* injuries (`Raise Dead`, `Apex Form`) or remove a condition *caused by* one (`Ongoing Care`). This is the only colour-boundary claim in the guide that survives intact.

**"Black isolates; Green clusters."** → **HALF TRUE.**
- Black *reads* Isolated in 5 talents but *creates* it in **one** (`Unnerving Approach`, a Free Action that pushes one enemy's ally away). `Dread Presence` maintains it. Meanwhile **deity/Chaos applies Isolated twice** (`Isolating Pressure`, `Isolating Ruin`) — more than Black does — and Chaos's entry gate is *Black 2+*, so the Black player is the one who buys the tree that out-isolates his own colour.
- Green "clusters" **allies**, not enemies. The guide is explicit that the mechanic is enemies: *"Difficult terrain + adjacency bonuses reward **enemies being together**. Green's cluster engine mirrors Black's isolation engine."* Exactly **one** Green talent pays off enemies being together — `Apex Predator` ("three or more enemies within your difficult terrain → advantage on all Physical tests"), depth 3, Green 3+, **earliest level 6**. Every other adjacency effect is ally-facing: `Pack Hunter` (you move adjacent to an enemy *an ally* is adjacent to), `Coordinated Hunt` (bonus = number of *different attackers*), `Pack Pressure`, `Pack Sense`, `Packmate's Warning`. Green is a **focus-fire** tree, which is a coherent and different thing.

**"Blue manipulates probability; Red escalates momentum."**
- Blue: manipulates probability *only* as disadvantage — `Pattern Recognition`, `Intercept`, `False Premise`, `Probability Cascade`, `Absolute Stillness` (5, the largest single-tree block in the game). Given the primer's binary-advantage fact, those 5 talents largely do **one talent's work** per round. Zero plot-die interaction.
- Red: **escalates, and this is the underrated true thing about the tree.** `Battle Fever` (+1 to your and every ally's next test per damage instance in Attunement Range, max = Rank, free, no action) and `Feeding Frenzy` (+1 when an enemy attacks another enemy) are **numeric stacking bonuses** — the only stacking bonuses in the leyline atlas, and therefore the only "more sources = more value" effects in a system where advantage is binary. Fast-turn synergy delivers too (`Burning Drive`, `Frenzied Tempo`, `Unstoppable`). What is **absent** is the guide's stated *mechanism*: "damage **taken** feeds future power." Battle Fever reads damage *dealt by anyone*. Zero Red talents read the caster's own wounds, missing HP, or HP as a cost.

The guide assigned Focus to two leyline colours and the data gave it to a different atlas.

### 3.3 Black's "Focus economy" — the sharpest single drift I found

Black's guide identity reads: *"Focus economy — **spend Focus for leverage, strip enemy Focus, dominate the depleted**."* The path description agrees: *"Focus stripped from the enemy mind."*

- **Spend Focus for leverage**: 1 talent (`Puppeteer`, 2 focus).
- **Strip enemy Focus**: **0 talents.** `Coercive Pressure` triggers *when* an enemy loses focus; `Whispered Doubt` makes them spend 1 more; `Siphoned Will` and `Predatory Insight` *regain* focus for you. None removes focus from an enemy.
- **Dominate the depleted**: `Puppeteer` — depth 4, Black 3+, **earliest L6**, Reaction, 2 focus + 1 Investiture, and it requires a target **at 0 focus**, a state Black cannot produce.

The whole loop is implemented end-to-end by **heroic/Warrior**, which never claims it: `Feinting Strike` (depth 1, L2 — melee attack vs. Cognitive, target loses focus equal to your Intimidation ranks) → `Wit's End` (depth 2, L3 — vs. a target at 0 focus, ignores Deflect, +4d6, cannot graze). Warrior strips it and executes; Black promises both and does neither.

### 3.5 Leyline structural benchmarks

3-Action talents: **0 in all five leyline trees** ✓ (rule 3 delivered; the two 3-Action talents outside deity are Agent's `Close the Case`/Hunter's `Tagging Shot`/Leader's two, all heroic). Description length: leyline means 21.2–24.7 words against a 20–25 target ✓ — the best-honoured numeric standard in the document.

## 5. Promises made in more than one tree's intent — where the overlap starts in the design docs

1. **The plot die is promised to three trees.** Blue's guide ("capstone identity"), White's guide ("Key mechanic: Plot Die integration"), and Agent's path prose ("grasp the threads of fate… waiting for the perfect moment to pull"). Delivered: Agent 8, White 4, Blue 0. Two of the three claims can coexist; the third was never built.
2. **Focus-stripping is promised to Black and delivered by Warrior.** Neither Warrior source mentions focus at all. See §3.3.
3. **Isolation is promised to Black and out-delivered by Chaos**, which gates on Black. The Black player buys both.
4. **Forced-action domination lives in three trees.** Red's `Incite` (forces a Strike on the nearest character), Black's `Hollow Command` + `Puppeteer`, Power's `Kneel` + `Absolute Authority`. Only Power's prose claims it as an identity; Black's claims it too ("commands the depleted cannot refuse") and reaches it four levels later behind a precondition it cannot meet.
5. **Six single-target mark subsystems**, and two of them are the same machine. Hunter's *quarry*, Knowledge's *Insight*, Life's *Diagnosed*, Chaos's *Omen*, Fate's *Hexmark*, Order's *Edict*. The collision is **Knowledge ↔ Life**: `Studied Mark` (1 Action, 1 Investiture, depth 0 — place a mark, learn HP + conditions + Physical and Spiritual defences) vs. `Vital Diagnosis` (1 Action, 1 Investiture, depth 0 — place a mark, learn HP + **max HP** + conditions + Physical and Spiritual defences, **and you and every ally deal +Tier Vital damage to it**). Life's entry does everything Knowledge's entry does, plus max HP, plus the party-damage rider that Knowledge charges a second talent (`Pack Share`, gated **Green 3+ / L6**) to get. The path descriptions sell one as a predator-scholar and one as a surgeon; mechanically the entry talents are the same card, and the surgeon's is better.
6. **Social leverage is promised to Leader, Envoy (Diplomat) and Agent (Spy)** — and the intent files themselves prescribe the multi-path overlap ("Leader and Scholar make excellent multi-path choices" in Envoy's own text). The three share `High Society Contacts` (verbatim, Agent + Envoy), `Well Dressed` (Envoy + Leader), `Customary Garb` (verbatim, Envoy + Leader) and `Baleful` (verbatim, Blue + Agent + Leader).
7. **Damage-redirection is implemented four times** — White's `Shared Burden`, Order's `Shoulder the Oath`, Power's `Mantle of the Aspirant`, Life's `Lifeline` — and only White's prose claims it ("damage sharing").
8. **Not a collision, and worth stating so:** the five zone/terrain trees (Green *difficult terrain* 6, Destruction *dangerous terrain* 8, Fate *Ordained Ground*/*Snare* 7, Civilization *Foundation* 5, Death *Bone Garden* 1) use **disjoint keywords with different mechanics**. This is the design working. Whatever is wrong with the ecosystem, terrain is not it.

**The shared-stock problem, measured.** 39 talent slots carry a name that exists in another tree; 36 carry byte-identical descriptions. The largest blocs are `Hardy` (7 trees), `Mighty` (6), `Collected` (5), the focus-cap talent (7 slots across 6 trees under **three different names** — `Composed`, `Focused Mind`, `Clear Mind`), `Baleful` (3), `Surefooted` (3). At 39–43 slots that is 11–12% of the corpus, and it is concentrated in heroic: 30 of the 39 duplicate slots are heroic talents. Every one of these is a slot that could have carried the specialty promise the tree is currently missing — `Mighty` sits in Envoy's **Mentor** branch and Agent's **Spy** branch doing nothing for either fantasy.

**▼ Scholar note.** Its something is real and uncontested, but it lies **entirely outside combat**, which is why it reads as weak at a battle map. Its *secondary* claim — healing — is genuinely contested and loses: Green heals more per Action and removes Injuries; Life heals more still. Scholar's healing claim survives only in the precise form "the best **non-Investiture** healer," which matters because an all-heroic party has no Investiture at all. That is worth writing into its prose, because right now nothing says it.

**⚠️ Sovereignty is the one tree where I would flag the "something."** The mechanic is unique, but three things are true at once and each is checkable:

1. **It is invisible at the table.** `Censure` (1 Action, 1 Investiture, contested test) steps one enemy's damage die down one, until the start of your next turn: against a d8 attacker, **−1 average damage per damage die**. The **capstone** `Sovereignty` (3 Actions, 3 Investiture, once per scene) is −2 steps for the scene: d8→d4, **−2 average per die**. Compare **White's `Shield Wall`** — depth 3, **no action, no cost, permanent, multi-target** — which reduces incoming damage by half [Tier][Die] = **4.5 average at Tier 2**. White's free passive out-mitigates Sovereignty's once-per-scene capstone by roughly 2×.
2. **Its elevate half is dominated one tree over.** `Exalt`: 1 Action + 1 Investiture, one ally, one die step, one round — roughly **+1 damage per hit**. `Investiture of Authority`: 2 Actions + 2 Investiture, gated **White 3+ / L6**, same +1 for the scene. deity/Knowledge's `The Pack`: 1 Action + 2 Investiture, **once**, and **every** ally in Attunement Range deals bonus Vital damage equal to the Insight count (up to 5) against the marked creature for the whole scene.
3. **There is no second thing.** 0 damage formulas, 0 heal formulas, 0 zones, 0 Reactions, 0 information, 0 social, 0 exploration, 0 summons — verified on both evidence streams. It is the only tree in the corpus of which all of that is simultaneously true.

**So the answer to the question that started this review is not the one the premise assumed.** The designer flagged White and Blue as the no-damage trees. Both have an uncontested something and neither is the problem: White owns the best free ally mitigation in the game and the plot-die coordination its guide promised, and Blue owns illusions plus the only counterspell. **The third no-damage tree, Sovereignty, is the one that cannot name a moment where a party would rather have it than the alternative** — and the reason is legible in the drift ledger: its compensating identity was supposed to be the Decree zone, and the Decree zone was never built.

A related correction for the designer's framing: **Blue and Scholar do not do the same thing.** Scholar has no illusion, no interrupt, no disadvantage engine, no zone; Blue has no crafting, no skill re-spec, no healing, no expertise grants. Their 0.794 cosine (rank **11** of 210) is an artefact of both being non-damage support trees; Blue's actual nearest neighbours are **Black (0.844)** and **Red (0.869)**, and what all three share is imposing conditions on a single target.

## 8. What to fix, sorted by failure mode

**UNDERDELIVERS — the fix is talents:**
- Sovereignty's **Decree zone** (the missing third step of a three-step loop the other two steps exist to feed). Highest priority in the corpus.
- Power's **Bounty** — re-scope `Warlord's Fury` to count kills and to feed the domination talents, or build the tally as a passive on an entry.
- Black's **focus-strip** — one talent that removes enemy focus turns `Puppeteer` from a dead capstone into a payoff, and reclaims an identity Warrior currently owns.
- Life's **`Adaptive Mutation` gate** — move it off Green 3+ so the signature resource exists at tier 1.
- Hunter's **Archer AoE**, Scholar's **military branch**, Civilization's **repair**, Envoy's **Faithful** religious mechanic. Four specialty slots; note that Envoy, Hunter and Scholar each carry 2–3 shared-stock talents (`Mighty`, `Hardy`, `Collected`, `Surefooted`) that could be spent on them at zero net slot cost.
- Anything at all that applies **Frightened**, or edit the two Power clauses that read it.

**COHERENT BUT DIFFERENT — the fix is prose:**
- Blue's guide entry: strike the plot-die "capstone identity" claim and write the disadvantage/illusion/interrupt identity the talents actually deliver. Give the plot-die language to White (which earned it) and to Agent.
- Red's guide entry: strike "HP loss" and "damage taken feeds future power"; write in the stacking-escalation identity that `Battle Fever` and `Feeding Frenzy` genuinely and uniquely deliver.
- Green's guide entry: "clusters" means **allies onto one target**, not enemies together. Either say so, or promote the one talent (`Apex Predator`) that does the stated thing out of the L6 slot.
- Chaos: the tree is a good mark-and-detonate nuker. Either rewrite Omen's source (placed by landing a test) and drop the focus/advantage spends, or build the Complication mechanic. Do not ship prose describing a third tree.
- Black's 10 ft → 5 ft in both intent sources.
- Warrior: write the **stance system** into the path description. It is the tree's best mechanic and no intent source mentions it.
- Scholar: say "the best healer who needs no Investiture," which is defensible, instead of implying a general healing claim that Green and Life beat.

**BOTH SOURCES NEED RECONCILING before either can be tested again:**
- The deity colour-thematic test rule (guide Part 3 §4) is falsified in **6 of 10 trees** (Order, Civilization, Fate, Life, Knowledge, Sovereignty) for the same structural reason: **the "lighter colour" half of those trees was designed with no test at all** — ally buffs, pacts, placements and heals just happen. The rule as written can never apply to an untested effect. Either the rule needs a magnitude clause ("if a talent does not test, the lighter colour must set one of its numbers" — which is exactly what Order and Civilization already do and what makes them the only two gates that aren't tolls), or five deity trees need a test added to their light half.

### What the analysis missed

- The Sovereignty capstone's second and third clauses. The analysis's most load-bearing conclusion turns on a mitigation comparison built from half a talent — it quotes the enemy's −2 die steps and silently drops both the ally's +2 steps and 'Whenever the ally hits the enemy, the enemy cannot take reactions until the start of its next turn', a scene-long repeatable reaction lock. Its own remit ('check every quotation is verbatim and says what the analysis says it says') targets exactly this.
- Speed's dual meaning in the corpus. One grep of 'Speed' across the 365 returns Red's OWN Unstoppable saying 'move up to half your Speed' — which would have flipped finding #4 from an implementation misreading to a design number. The analysis asserted 'Speed is an attribute (1–4) in this system' without checking whether the corpus ever uses it that way. It does not, anywhere.
- Green's Coordinated Hunt as the counterexample to Red's stacking exclusivity — a talent the analysis quotes elsewhere in the same document (in §3.1, discussing Green's 'cluster' claim) without noticing it is the shape it declares unique to Red one section earlier.
- Studied Mark's Insight placement. The Knowledge↔Life overlap is presented as 'the same card, and the surgeon's is better' while omitting the two words that carry Knowledge's entire damage engine, and comparing against Pack Share (L6, +Tier) rather than The Pack (depth 2 / L3, up to +5).
- Applying its own three-column test (tests + dice + magnitude) to gate colours it did not already suspect. Having correctly diagnosed that the census's classifier cannot see magnitude terms, it rebuilt the table only for the accused rows — so Civilization's Red (a DC in Bastion and Magnum Opus), Destruction's Red and Fate's Green are still scored by the census's method inside the analysis's own corrected table.
- Sovereignty's earliest levels. It calls Sovereignty 'the most severely drifted' and never states that seven of its nine talents are reachable by L3 and only two are L6-gated — the tree is front-loaded by level, which is the fact that makes its weak entries decisive rather than incidental.
- Internal consistency: the ledger table and the totals line disagree about whether Order and Destruction are DELIVERS or near-DELIVERS, and the plot-die headline (18) does not match its own table (17).

### Surviving findings, in priority order

1. PRIORITY 1 — Sovereignty is still the tree without a moment, but for a different reason than the analysis gave. Its FLOOR is the worst per-action rate in the deity atlas: Exalt (1 Action + 1 Investiture, one ally, +1 average damage, one round) and Censure (1 Action + 1 Investiture + a contested Black test, −1 average per die, one round) against Knowledge's Predatory Strike and Life's Vital Diagnosis at identical cost and identical depth 0 / L1. Its CEILING is fine — the capstone at depth 3 / L4 is a scene-long ±2-step swing plus a repeatable 'the enemy cannot take reactions' lock. Fix the entries, not the capstone; and the missing Decree zone is still the cleanest single explanation for why the two halves have nowhere to go.
2. PRIORITY 2 — Momentum's Edge is a live balance bug, not a wording bug, and it is worse than the ledger says. 'Speed' means movement-in-feet in Red's own Unstoppable and in six other talents, and is never a 1–4 adder anywhere in the corpus. A free depth-2 Passive delivering ~+20–30 flat impact damage cannot be talked down by rewriting the card; the number itself has to change.
3. PRIORITY 3 — Power's Bounty and Sovereignty's Decree are both absent, and Power additionally carries two permanently dead clauses (Kneel and Absolute Authority both read Frightened, which no talent in 365 produces). Confirmed on every axis. Warlord's Fury's cap of tier×2 means the whole 'snowball' is +2 at levels 1–5.
4. PRIORITY 4 — The absolute toll booths are three, not five, and the analysis's correction of the census is right: Fate/White 2+, Knowledge/Green 3+ and Sovereignty/White 3+ buy zero tests, zero dice and zero magnitude terms, while Order/White and Civilization/White carry real numeric magnitude (Bear Witness, Shoulder the Oath ×2 terms, Bonds of Community).
