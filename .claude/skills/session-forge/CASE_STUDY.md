# Case study — the session-1 build ("The Harvest That Won't Die", 2026-07-12/13, PR #75)

The build this skill is distilled from, told as the six catches that made the difference.
Read this before drafting; every phase in SKILL.md exists because one of these happened.

## 1. The convoy that forded its own delivery route (geography before fiction)

First draft: a cart convoy carries relief grain from Elmsworth to Withervale and gets ambushed
"at the ford." Ben's review: *both towns are in Thalendor on the same river — what are we
fording? The river IS the delivery system.* The fiction had been written before the geography
was checked. The fix made the scene **better**: a barge flotilla, ambushed at the braided
shallows that are simultaneously the slow single-file channel AND the only wadeable border
crossing for fifty miles — the geography now *forces* the scene (that's why the raiders are
there and nowhere else). Lesson: when logistics contradict the fiction, the corrected logistics
usually hand you a stronger scene than the one you invented.

## 2. "It's a day" — for 1,339 km (measure, never estimate)

The same draft had Khor say the trip was a day. Ben's scale ruling (4,000 km north–south →
1 px ≈ 1.5 km) made it ~430 km straight-line — and when the channel was actually traced, the
drawn river meandered ~2.1×: **1,339 km, twelve days at barge pace**. Every travel number in the
final sheet is a `measure.py` output. Twelve days also *improved* the session: room for travel
beats, an escort job that earns its pay, a world that feels its size. Lesson: run Phase 2 before
any number appears in prose, and treat surprising measurements as material, not obstacles.

## 3. The Joskin-decapitation test (stress-test the premise)

Ben asked: *what happens if a player cuts off the dying elder's head?* The session's central
image — the man who can't finish dying — had no canon answer for the obvious player response.
That one probe produced the death-mechanics ruling (steel works; the wasting crawls), the
mercy-kill contingency (the script's strongest scene), and the corpse-piles scale ruling. A
session premise the players can poke a hole in *will* be poked at the table. Lesson: Phase 3 is
cheaper than a mid-session canon improvisation that next session has to honor.

## 4. One menu, recommended defaults (batch the rulings)

Every design question the build surfaced went to Ben as a single `AskUserQuestion` batch with a
recommended default first — death mechanics, corpse scale, raid context, journey length in one
menu; river pacing, barge speed, river name in another. Ben answered seven world-defining
questions in two sittings without the build ever stalling. The answers that changed world truth
were logged to canon §9 the same day. Lesson: don't dribble, don't decide silently, don't stall.

## 5. The un-missable clue (Malcurr's maker's-marks)

The campaign's first political thread hangs on players noticing the raiders' gear is too good.
A Perception DC could delete the thread on a bad roll — so the sheet adds the fallback: looting
any downed raider puts the Malcurr-stamped blade *in a PC's hand*. Lesson: campaign-gating clues
get a delivery path that cannot fail; rolls modulate *when*, never *whether*.

## 6. The parallel-session collision (sync before writing)

While this branch built session 1, another session merged a lore batch to main — ruling the SAME
death-mechanics question under the SAME §9 numbers, and proposing a Withervale priest beat that
contradicted the run-sheet's Harrow. The merge cost a careful reconciliation: main's richer
consent-model kept as canonical, this branch's rulings renumbered 18–23, the two Harrow
characterizations composed into one arc (he HAS given the harvest by hand; Joskin is where his
nerve broke), and the composition ⚑-flagged rather than silently decided. Lesson: Phase 0's
merged-main check is not bureaucracy; shared surfaces (rulings numbers, delta ids, NPC
characterizations) drift fast when sessions run in parallel — and when two Ben-approved truths
conflict, *compose or ask*, never pick silently.

## What the finished sheet did right (copy these)

- §0 separated **the job** (escort grain) from **the point** (feel that nothing can die) and
  wrote the do-NOT-reveal wall before any scene.
- The fight was **sad, not evil** — Roek surrenders, mercy earns a Corvaine contact — because
  canon says the raiders are desperate, and tone is a canon fact.
- The session **ends quiet**, on the point, with the cow still breathing at dawn.
- The clue ledger made the session auditable: five planted clues, each mapped to the thread it
  feeds, plus an explicit stays-buried list — which is exactly what the debrief reconciles
  against after play.
