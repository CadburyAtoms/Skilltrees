## 1. The hook

Start with the famine — but make the first **clue** the broken death-cycle, not the hunger.
The famine is what everyone in Thalendor already knows; the hook is the moment the PCs see the
famine is a *symptom of something theologically impossible*: **nothing here can properly die.**

The image the whole campaign hangs on, planted in scene 1 and never explained early:

- Blighted crops that **stand without rotting**.
- A plague-stricken elder who has been "dying" for months and *can't finish*.
- Livestock too weak to stand that won't lie down and die.
- A village shrine of the Last Harvest gone silent — prayers for a merciful end unanswered.

Per canon (§2–§3), all of it traces to Morrath's sealing — the single root cause of the
continental crisis — and when Anaveth eventually says "Death is sealed," the entire first arc
clicks into place retroactively.

### Why the hook survives unknown player choices

- **Any origin justifies being in Thalendor.** Every nation has a push factor: plague (Malcurr,
  Corvaine), war (Vorsk), famine (Lunavar). Refugees, mercenaries, traders, and pilgrims all
  plausibly wash up in the one utopia still (barely) feeding people. Natives work trivially.
- **Any build fits a convoy escort** — fighters guard, healers tend, talkers negotiate, sneaks
  scout.
- **Every leyline color gets an early tug** — Green PCs *feel* the Root Network being drained;
  Black PCs sense entropy that never resolves; Blue/White PCs notice the pattern doesn't add up.
  Improvise a "your power flickers oddly near the village" beat for whatever the players bring.

## 2. Session 1 — the relief convoy

> NPCs with faces, the ford combat statted, and a clue ledger. This section is the outline; the
> run-sheet is what you read at the table.

The PCs are attached (hired, conscripted by **Marshal Vareth Khor**, or simply paid in food) to
escort a **barge flotilla** of the Mage's alchemical relief concentrate from a granary staging
town to a starving village on the Corvaine border — **~430 km, 5–6 days downriver**; the border
river IS the delivery system (scale: canon §5a — 1 px ≈ 1.5 km, ~80 km/day downriver).

Coordinates are keyed to the ground-truth map (canon §5a): sites run **south down the
Thalendor/Corvaine border** — the great meandering river on the east edge of the central forest —
ending at the Black Altar Crossing where the Thalendor/Corvaine/Canticle borders meet.

### Battle-map briefs (road-level, drawable now)

1. **Palewater Ford** (~30×20 squares). The barge channel snakes corner-to-corner through braided
   gravel shallows; three barges strung single file, the lead one grounded on a bar as the
   chokepoint. Willow scrub on the Corvaine bank for the crossbow line; gravel-bar edges give
   half-cover; the knee-deep shallows are difficult terrain for everyone; the channel itself is
   swimming-deep. Design goal: the PCs defend three slow-moving objects in a kill-box while
   skirmish lines wade in. (Full scene: `EDHA_SESSION_1_SCRIPT.md` §3.)
2. **Withervale village** (~30×30). Village green with the Last Harvest shrine at center; empty
   granary; the elder's house; a blighted field on the outskirts where the crops stand unrotting
   (creepy set-dressing, and a night-encounter space if wanted). Mostly a social/exploration map.
3. **Black Altar Crossing** (~40×30, act-1 finale). The river-nexus where the great meandering
   river gathers at the forest's southeast tip: a natural stone causeway, the basalt altar
   platform on an islet mid-channel, destabilizing-leyline visual effects. Three borders
   (**Thalendor / Corvaine / Canticle**) meet here — which is *why* it's called a Crossing and why
   three nations' problems converge on it.

## 3. Act structure — famine → gods → the Fetch

The canon's **assembly rule** (§2) is the reveal structure: no single being sees the whole
picture; the players become the only ones who have spoken to everyone. The Fetch is uncovered by
**assembly**, not one dramatic reveal.

1. **Act 1 — mortal scale.** The famine is a symptom. Following the raiders' funding leads
   toward Malcurr and the Warlock; following the blight leads to the drained Root Network. Both
   teach the same lesson: the crisis is top-down. Finale at the **Black Altar Crossing**, where
   the divine layer becomes undeniable.
2. **First god contact — Anaveth** (canon: the most accessible god; panicking, wants help, talks
   to anyone capable). Route: Goldenport's suspicious prosperity next to the famine is the natural
   investigative pull → **Serene** → Anaveth. She delivers the first divine-scale fact —
   **Morrath is missing; Life is drowning without Death** — and the mission spine: find out how,
   undo it.
3. **Act 2 — the false villain.** Let the players conclude **Tyrith did it**. Olvarra genuinely
   believes it; Tessavain independently confirms a coup is coming; all the honest evidence points
   at Power. The party spends an arc fighting Tyrith's mortal machinery — real, dangerous, worth
   stopping — and it is exactly where the Fetch wants them looking.
4. **The assembly — three pieces that only combine in the players' hands:**
   - **Miravel** (Kettavar): Chaos *feels wrong* — "a river that only flows one direction" —
     texture without framework.
   - **Canticle's archives**: pre-infiltration Chaos theology — a god whose schemes never served
     anything and never *finished*. The Bards don't know what they have.
   - **Olvarra**: sees the strategy perfectly, pins it on the wrong god.
   Combined tell (canon ruling 4): **a god of the broken plan should never have a plan that
   finishes — and this one's plans finish.** The reveal should happen *at the table*, players
   connecting it themselves.
5. **Act 3 — the follow-ons.** Telling Olvarra the truth means telling her that her own coup
   opened the door — guilt that makes her the most motivated ally. Razkael (breadcrumb:
   Commander Isra Vael, Vorsk) is the only direct witness worth hunting down. Unsealing Morrath —
   mechanism unknown even to the gods — is the endgame.
